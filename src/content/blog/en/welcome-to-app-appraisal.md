Every valuation ends in a single number. What separates a number a bank will lend
against from a number that gets sent back is not the arithmetic — it is everything
that sits behind it.

## The standard comes first

We apply the **International Valuation Standards (IVS)** and the **RICS Red Book**
to every engagement, whatever the size of the asset. That is not a marketing line;
it changes the work. The standard dictates how the basis of value is chosen, what
evidence must be inspected, how assumptions are disclosed, and who reviews the file
before it is signed.

It also means the report can be read outside Cambodia. A credit committee in
Singapore, an auditor applying IFRS, or a counterparty in a cross-border
transaction all recognise the framework immediately.

## Then the evidence

A standard tells you how to work. It cannot tell you what a property in Tuol Kork
is worth. That comes from transaction evidence — and in a market where much of that
evidence is never formally published, the depth of your own database is the
difference between an opinion and a conclusion.

## And finally, the signature

A valuation carries the name of the person who prepared it. Ours are scoped
directly with the valuer who will sign the report, not routed through an account
manager. If a figure is ever questioned, the person who reached it is the person
who explains it.
